# Sakhi Clinic 2.0 - Doctor Home Experience

## Status
- Version: 1.0
- Scope: Doctor Home Screen experience only
- Design intent: Experience design for the doctor’s daily starting point, not visual system definition
- Constraint: No colors, typography, or components are defined here

## 1. Objectives
The Doctor Home Screen exists to help the doctor understand the state of the clinic in under three seconds and move into the next clinical action with minimal friction.

Primary objectives:
- Help the doctor immediately understand today’s clinic load, urgency, and readiness
- Surface the next most valuable action without forcing navigation
- Reduce cognitive load during a busy clinical day
- Preserve trust by making state, progress, and risk visible at all times
- Support both planned consultations and unexpected interruptions

## 2. Doctor Goals
The experience should support these doctor goals:
- Know what is happening today before anything else
- Identify patients who need attention first
- Start consultation flow quickly and confidently
- Review pending tasks without losing context
- Stay informed when the clinic is busy, delayed, or offline
- Delegate or request help when appropriate without breaking workflow

## 3. Information Hierarchy
The information hierarchy must be simple, calm, and clinically useful.

### Highest priority
1. Today’s clinic status
   - How busy the day is
   - How many patients are waiting or due soon
   - Whether there are urgent items that need action
2. Immediate next step
   - The one action that matters most right now
   - The action should be obvious without reading the whole screen
3. Patient context readiness
   - Who is ready, who is delayed, and who needs review

### Secondary priority
4. Upcoming schedule and flow health
   - Current queue state
   - Delays, no-shows, or priority changes
5. Help and support signals
   - AI assistance availability
   - System status or connectivity state
6. Review and follow-up tasks
   - Items that can be handled later without blocking current care

## 4. Screen Zones
The screen should be organized into clear zones so the doctor can scan it quickly.

### Zone A: Clinic snapshot
A compact, high-clarity summary of today’s clinical state.
- Total patients expected today
- Patients currently waiting or in progress
- Urgent or overdue items
- Overall readiness of the clinic

### Zone B: Primary action surface
The most important action area for the doctor’s next move.
- Start consultation
- Resume a patient in progress
- Review the highest-priority queue item
- Continue a pending task

### Zone C: Queue and workflow stream
A live, structured list of patients or tasks in the order they matter.
- Current priority sequence
- Waiting patients
- In-progress patients
- Follow-up or review items

### Zone D: Support and context zone
A calm companion area for intelligent assistance and clinical context.
- AI assistant suggestions
- Voice command availability
- Helpful context such as “patient is late,” “lab result pending,” or “next step recommended”

### Zone E: System state footer
A quiet but trustworthy status area.
- Connection status
- Sync state
- Pending saves or background processing
- Offline or degraded-mode indication

## 5. Primary Actions
Primary actions must be obvious, fast, and clinically meaningful.

The primary actions on the Doctor Home Screen are:
- Start or resume consultation
- Move to the next patient in priority order
- Review urgent or delayed patient needs
- Confirm the next action for the current clinic state

These actions should be available without requiring the doctor to search or navigate deeply.

## 6. Secondary Actions
Secondary actions should support the workflow without competing with core tasks.

Examples:
- View full queue
- Review patient history
- Open notes or plans
- Access follow-up tasks
- View AI suggestions
- Open settings or system status
- Trigger voice help

Secondary actions must remain available, but they must never overshadow the doctor’s next clinical move.

## 7. AI Assistant Behaviour
The AI assistant should behave like a calm clinical co-pilot, not a separate product experience.

### Behaviour principles
- The assistant should offer support only when it reduces friction or improves clarity
- It should surface helpful suggestions, not force interaction
- It should clearly distinguish between observed information, inferred insight, and suggested next actions
- It should never present uncertainty as certainty
- It should be easy to dismiss, ignore, or refine

### Expected assistance patterns
- “This patient has been waiting 20 minutes”
- “Two patients are due for follow-up in the next hour”
- “The next likely action is to review medication adherence”
- “An appointment may be delayed because the previous consultation is running long”

The assistant should be subtle, trusted, and easy to skip.

## 8. Voice Integration
Voice should feel like a support layer for busy clinical work, not a separate mode.

### Voice experience principles
- Voice should enable hands-free action when appropriate
- The doctor should be able to ask for the next patient, current queue state, or a summary of urgent items
- Voice should support confirmation for safe actions when needed
- The system must clearly signal listening, processing, and pause states
- The experience must include a non-voice fallback for every core task
- Voice should never feel mandatory

### Voice use cases
- “Show me the next priority patient”
- “What needs attention now?”
- “Start consultation for patient A”
- “Summarize today’s urgent items”

## 9. One-handed Interaction
The experience must work well in stressed, interrupted, and one-handed conditions.

### Interaction expectations
- Primary actions should be reachable with a thumb-friendly path
- The doctor should not need to type unless necessary
- The interface should support quick taps, swipes, and short confirmations
- Important states should remain readable even when the user is interrupted or distracted
- Repeated actions should feel predictable and low-friction

### One-handed design intent
- The doctor can understand the screen and act without needing to explore deeply
- The most important action should be within the first visible interaction layer
- Secondary tasks should remain available but should not interrupt the core workflow

## 10. Trust Indicators
Trust is created through visibility, consistency, and calm communication.

The screen should clearly communicate:
- Whether data has been saved
- Whether the system is currently processing something
- Whether there is a connection issue or reduced capability
- Whether a recommendation is AI-generated or based on known clinical context
- Whether the doctor’s current action is safe to proceed

The experience should never leave the doctor wondering whether the clinic state is current.

## 11. Offline Behaviour
The Doctor Home Screen must remain usable under degraded or no-connectivity conditions.

### Offline expectations
- The doctor should still understand the current clinic state as last known
- The system must clearly indicate that some information may be stale or pending sync
- Core queue and patient actions should remain accessible where possible
- The screen should offer the next best safe action when full network functionality is unavailable
- The experience must avoid creating false confidence about live updates

## 12. Empty States
Empty states should reduce anxiety and guide the doctor clearly.

### Empty or quiet clinic state
If there is no urgent work, the experience should communicate calm clarity instead of emptiness.
- “No patients need immediate attention”
- “The clinic is currently stable”
- “Next consultation is scheduled shortly”

The system should avoid making the screen feel blank, broken, or uncertain.

## 13. Error States
Errors should be visible, calm, and actionable.

Examples:
- A patient record could not be loaded
- Sync failed for a recent update
- The AI suggestion is unavailable
- A queue item has become inconsistent

Error behaviour should:
- Explain what happened in plain language
- State what the doctor can do next
- Avoid alarming the user unnecessarily
- Preserve the current clinical context where possible

## 14. Loading States
Loading states should reduce uncertainty and preserve confidence.

### Required behaviour
- Loading should feel purposeful and brief
- The system should communicate progress instead of leaving the screen blank
- The doctor should know whether the system is preparing data, syncing, or waiting for a response
- The next best action should remain available if possible

## 15. Accessibility
Accessibility is a clinical safety requirement.

The experience must support:
- Low-light and stressful use
- One-handed and interrupted interaction
- Clear reading without over-reliance on color
- Immediate recognition of system state and urgency
- Assistive technology use without unnecessary complexity
- Clear labels and predictable action patterns

The system should remain understandable even when the doctor is rushed, fatigued, or multitasking.

## 16. Clinical Rationale
The Doctor Home Screen is not merely an overview page; it is a decision-support surface for daily clinic control.

The experience should make it easy for the doctor to answer three questions instantly:
1. What is happening today?
2. What needs attention first?
3. What should I do next?

If those questions are answered quickly and clearly, the doctor can move into care with less friction and greater confidence.

## 17. Acceptance Criteria
The Doctor Home Experience is considered complete when:
- A doctor can understand the day’s clinic state within three seconds
- The primary next action is obvious without searching
- Urgent, waiting, and in-progress patients are distinguishable
- AI assistance is available but clearly secondary to doctor judgment
- Voice assistance is available and recoverable
- The experience remains usable in one-handed, offline, and degraded conditions
- Empty, loading, and error states are calm and actionable
- The experience feels trustworthy, calm, and clinically useful
