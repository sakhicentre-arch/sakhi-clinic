# Sakhi Clinic 2.0 - Master Patient Journey

## Status
- Version: 1.0
- Scope: End-to-end patient lifecycle experience
- Design intent: Backbone experience definition for the product
- Constraint: No screens, no components, and no visual design are defined here

## 1. Purpose
This document defines the complete patient experience lifecycle as a continuous clinical journey. It is the backbone for all future experience design work and establishes the expected flow from first discovery through long-term history and eventual archival.

The journey is:

Patient discovered
↓
Patient registered
↓
Waiting
↓
Consultation
↓
Voice capture
↓
AI processing
↓
Prescription
↓
Payment
↓
Follow-up
↓
Long-term history
↓
Reactivation
↓
Archival

## 2. Doctor Goals
The doctor needs the experience to:
- Understand the patient’s current status without losing context
- Move quickly from arrival to consultation to follow-up
- Preserve clinical focus during busy periods
- Maintain trust in what the system captured and what it suggested
- Reduce repetitive documentation work
- Continue care across time without losing continuity

## 3. Patient Goals
The patient needs the experience to:
- Feel welcomed and understood
- Experience minimal friction from arrival to completion
- Trust that their history is respected and preserved
- Receive clear next steps without confusion
- Feel that the consultation is attentive and not distracted by technology
- Leave with confidence in the care plan and follow-up process

## 4. Core Experience Principles
The journey must feel:
- Calm rather than fragmented
- Continuous rather than stepwise and mechanical
- Trustworthy at every handoff
- Clinically meaningful rather than administrative
- Supportive of both doctor and patient without becoming the center of attention

## 5. Journey Stages

### Stage 1: Patient Discovered
The experience begins when a patient becomes relevant to the clinic system.

This may occur through:
- Direct appointment booking
- Walk-in arrival
- Referral intake
- Follow-up request
- Previous patient reactivation

The system should help the clinic understand that a patient is now part of the active care journey.

### Stage 2: Patient Registered
Registration should make the patient feel known and prepared without creating unnecessary friction.

The experience should support:
- Fast capture of essential identity and context
- Recognition of returning patients
- Preservation of prior history and preferences
- Clear indication of what is missing and what can be completed later

### Stage 3: Waiting
The waiting phase should feel structured and calm rather than uncertain.

The experience should support:
- Clear awareness of who is waiting and why
- Visibility of delay or readiness status
- A calm handoff into consultation when the doctor is ready
- Reduced anxiety for the patient and reduced overload for the doctor

### Stage 4: Consultation
The consultation is the center of the experience.

The experience should support:
- Immediate patient context access
- Minimal interruption to doctor-patient interaction
- Support for voice-led capture and quick review
- Smooth transition from conversation to structured clinical action

### Stage 5: Voice Capture
Voice capture should reduce the burden of typing and preserve attention on the patient.

The experience should support:
- Clear indication when recording is active
- Easy correction and review of captured content
- A natural flow between speaking, listening, and checking what was recorded
- Confidence that the system is capturing what matters without replacing the doctor’s judgment

### Stage 6: AI Processing
AI should process captured information into useful structure without becoming the authority in the room.

The experience should support:
- Clear distinction between raw capture, AI interpretation, and doctor decision
- Review and approval of AI-generated structure
- Confidence visibility for suggestions and uncertainty
- Safe handling of incomplete or ambiguous information

### Stage 7: Prescription
Prescription is a high-trust and high-safety moment.

The experience should support:
- Clear review of any recommendation before action
- Strong confirmation patterns for clinical decisions
- Protection against ambiguity or accidental action
- Preservation of doctor authority over final prescribing decisions

### Stage 8: Payment
Payment should feel simple, professional, and low-friction.

The experience should support:
- Clear completion status
- Minimal effort for the patient and doctor
- Strong confirmation and receipts
- Safe handling of failures or interruptions

### Stage 9: Follow-up
Follow-up should feel like a continuation of care rather than a new administrative task.

The experience should support:
- Clear next-step communication
- Timely scheduling or reminder logic
- Continuity of care across appointments and time
- Low effort for both doctor and patient to confirm what happens next

### Stage 10: Long-term History
Long-term history must preserve the patient’s story over time.

The experience should support:
- Easy access to past encounters without fragmentation
- Visible continuity across consultations
- Recognition of patterns, recurrence, and treatment history
- A reliable historical record that supports future decision-making

### Stage 11: Reactivation
A previous patient should be able to re-enter the system without feeling like they are starting over.

The experience should support:
- Recognition of prior context
- Immediate awareness of prior care and pending follow-up
- Minimal re-explanation and minimal friction
- Respect for continuity of care

### Stage 12: Archival
Archival is the closing of the active lifecycle and should feel complete and safe.

The experience should support:
- Clear completion status for the patient journey
- Preservation of records without confusion
- Secure and understandable transition into historical status
- Reliable access for future care if needed

## 6. Doctor Opportunities for AI
AI should create leverage at the moments that most burden the doctor.

High-value opportunities:
- Summarizing prior history before the consultation
- Structuring voice capture into usable clinical notes
- Highlighting follow-up needs and risks
- Suggesting prescription or scheduling options for review
- Supporting continuity across long-term history

AI must remain clearly assistive and reviewable.

## 7. Voice Opportunities
Voice should reduce friction where typing interrupts care.

High-value opportunities:
- Capture consultation details while maintaining eye contact
- Support hands-free navigation during busy moments
- Shorten repetitive documentation work
- Support review and confirmation of important actions

Voice should not replace doctor judgment or introduce ambiguity.

## 8. Trust Opportunities
Trust must be preserved at every transition in the journey.

The experience should make it clear:
- What was captured
- What was saved
- What AI processed
- What still needs review or confirmation
- What the next step is

Trust should be reinforced through clarity, continuity, and visible system state.

## 9. Failure Scenarios
The experience must anticipate the moments where the journey breaks down.

Examples:
- Patient data is incomplete
- Voice capture fails or is partial
- AI output is uncertain or low confidence
- Network connectivity drops mid-flow
- A prescription or payment action is interrupted
- Follow-up details are not confirmed
- A patient returns after a long gap

Each failure scenario should preserve patient safety and clinical continuity.

## 10. Recovery Paths
The experience must provide graceful recovery for interruptions and errors.

Recovery expectations:
- Return to the same point of context without losing the patient story
- Preserve what was already captured and saved
- Offer a clear next action after failure
- Avoid forcing the doctor to restart the journey unnecessarily
- Make uncertainty understandable without creating alarm

## 11. Offline Behaviour
The journey must remain usable under limited or unavailable connectivity.

Offline expectations:
- The doctor should still understand the current patient status
- The system should clearly indicate that some information may be pending sync
- Critical actions should remain safe and recoverable
- The experience should never imply that data has been fully synchronized when it has not

## 12. Acceptance Criteria
The Master Patient Journey is considered complete when:
- The full lifecycle from discovery to archival is clearly represented as one continuous experience
- Doctor goals, patient goals, AI opportunities, voice opportunities, trust opportunities, failure scenarios, recovery paths, offline behaviour, and acceptance criteria are all addressed
- The experience supports continuity of care across the full journey
- The journey is clinically coherent, trust-preserving, and low-friction
- The document is suitable as the backbone for future experience design documents

## 13. Self-Review
This document defines the experience backbone without converging on screens or components. It captures the full lifecycle, preserves clinical and trust requirements, and creates a clear foundation for later experience design work. The scope is appropriate and the document is sufficiently structured to guide future design decisions.
