# Sakhi Clinic 2.0 - Doctor Usability Test Plan

## Status
- Version: 1.0
- Phase: Experience Validation
- Purpose: Structured validation of real doctor usage in live clinical conditions
- Scope: Observation-based usability study, not redesign

## 1. Objectives
This study exists to understand how doctors actually use the product during real consultations and to collect evidence that can support future product decisions.

Primary objectives:
- Observe real doctor behaviour during live clinical use
- Identify where the experience helps or hinders care delivery
- Capture friction, hesitation, confusion, recovery behaviour, and errors
- Separate facts, observations, assumptions, opinions, and recommendations
- Establish evidence for future product prioritisation

## 2. Scope
The study will focus on the doctor experience across the core clinical journey, including:
- Patient discovery and search
- Registration and intake
- Waiting and queue awareness
- Consultation flow
- Voice capture and review
- AI-supported actions
- Prescription flow
- Payment flow
- Follow-up and history review
- Offline or degraded conditions where they occur naturally

The study will not attempt to redesign the product during the observation period.

## 3. Testing Methodology
### Study type
- In-situ usability observation during real clinical workflow
- Contextual inquiry with minimal interruption
- Observation of natural behaviour in real consultation conditions

### Study participants
- Primary participants: practising doctors using the product in actual clinical work
- Secondary participants: patients and support staff where relevant and appropriate

### Study setting
- Doctor’s real consultation environment
- Mobile device in normal usage context
- Real workflow, real time pressure, real interruptions

### Observation approach
- Observe without steering the doctor unless safety or consent requires intervention
- Capture behaviour and verbalised thinking where possible
- Keep the doctor focused on care first
- Avoid influencing the interaction unless a clinical or ethical issue requires it

## 4. Observation Rules
Observers must follow these rules:
- Do not interrupt the doctor during a sensitive clinical moment unless necessary
- Do not provide instructions or hints during tasks unless the doctor explicitly requests help
- Do not correct behaviour during observation
- Do not assume intent from a completed task without supporting evidence
- Record behaviour as seen, not as interpreted too early
- Distinguish between hesitation, confusion, delay, and deliberate review
- Capture both successful and unsuccessful moments

## 5. Success Criteria
The study will be considered successful if it produces evidence for:
- Where the experience supports clinical efficiency
- Where the experience creates avoidable effort or confusion
- Where doctors rely on voice, AI, or manual workarounds
- Where trust is strengthened or weakened
- Where the product performs well under interruption, time pressure, and partial connectivity

## 6. Duration
Recommended study duration:
- 2 to 4 consultation sessions per doctor, depending on availability
- 30 to 60 minutes of active observation per session
- Additional debrief time after the session for structured notes and clarification

## 7. Data Collection Methods
The study will use multiple evidence sources:
- Direct observation
- Time-based task notes
- Tapped-action counts where feasible
- Notes on scrolling, typing, voice use, hesitation, and recovery
- Post-session verbal reflection
- Structured feedback forms after observation
- Incident logs for errors, failures, and interruptions

No questionnaires are included in this document yet.

## 8. Testing Ethics
The study will be conducted with respect for clinical work, patient dignity, and professional autonomy.

Ethical principles:
- Participation must be voluntary
- The doctor may pause or withdraw at any time
- Observation must not interfere with patient care
- No personal or clinical data should be recorded beyond what is necessary for research purposes
- Findings must be used to improve the product experience, not to judge the doctor’s competence

## 9. Patient Privacy Considerations
Patient privacy is a core requirement.

Rules:
- Do not collect identifiable patient information unless strictly necessary and approved
- Avoid recording sensitive consultation details beyond what is needed for product validation
- Use anonymised or paraphrased notes wherever possible
- Do not capture private patient data in research notes or shared files
- Ensure all observation materials are handled with care and stored securely

## 10. Doctor Interruption Policy
Observation will be paused or stopped if:
- The doctor is in a sensitive or urgent clinical moment
- The patient requires uninterrupted attention
- The doctor indicates that observation is distracting or inappropriate
- A safety concern arises

The study must never compromise the consultation.

## 11. What Not to Do During Observation
Observers must not:
- Ask the doctor to perform artificial tasks outside normal workflow
- Prompt the doctor to use features they would not normally use
- Interpret confusion as a design failure without corroborating evidence
- Introduce new instructions during active care
- Record unnecessary patient details
- Suggest UI changes during observation
- Turn the session into a training exercise

## 12. Expected Outputs
The study should produce:
- A structured observation record for each consultation
- A set of observed pain points and success moments
- Evidence of where the product supports or obstructs clinical flow
- A list of factual findings that can be used for future design decisions
- A validated backlog of improvement opportunities, if evidence warrants it later

## 13. Self Review
This plan is research-oriented and intentionally avoids redesign. It focuses on observing real clinical behaviour, protecting the doctor and patient, and collecting evidence for future product decisions.
